I recently had the misfortune of stumbling on [Drew DeVault's](https://drewdevault.com/weird-guys/) list I decided to write one inspired by [Jette Steckel](https://www.muenchner-kammerspiele.de/de/wir/13725-jette-steckel). Everyone this one is a writer, poet, journalist, publisher, scholar or lawyer whom a government put in prison, or tried to, for something they wrote or said. Each entry links to its sources, and it was current in the first week of September 2026. Request additions, removals, or changes [via email](mailto:contact@hilll.dev); patches welcome.

## Alaa Abd El-Fattah

![Alaa Abd El-Fattah](/misc/media/writers-in-prison/abdelfattah.jpg)

**Country**: Egypt

**Known for**: Software developer, blogger, and author of _You Have Not Yet Been Defeated_

**Years in prison**: Multiple periods since 2011; most recently about six years from Sep 2019 to Sep 2025

**Current status**: Released in Sep 2025; allowed to leave Egypt in Dec 2025

**Imprisoned for**: Protest activity and sharing a Facebook post

Alaa Abd El-Fattah is a software developer, blogger and author of _You Have Not Yet Been Defeated_, and Egypt has imprisoned him under every head of state it has had since 2011. He was jailed under the military council in 2011, under the anti-protest law in 2013, for five years from 2014, and again from September 2019 [[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaa_Abd_El-Fattah)]. The last sentence, handed down in December 2021, was five years for sharing a Facebook post, and it was extended past its own end date by not counting the two years he had already served waiting for it. He obtained British citizenship through his mother in 2021, went on hunger strike for most of 2022 and stopped drinking water while COP27 was held in Sharm el-Sheikh, and his mother Laila Soueif spent nine months on hunger strike outside the Foreign Office in London. Sisi pardoned him on 22 September 2025 and he was released the next day. Egypt then refused to let him leave until December 2025; he arrived in Britain on 26 December.

## Ales Bialiatski

![Ales Bialiatski](/misc/media/writers-in-prison/bialiatski.jpg)

**Country**: Belarus

**Known for**: Literary scholar and founder of the Viasna human rights centre

**Years in prison**: 2011-2014, then Jul 2021-Dec 2025

**Current status**: Released and transferred to Lithuania in Dec 2025

**Imprisoned for**: Tax evasion, then smuggling and financing protests

Ales Bialiatski is a literary scholar who founded the Viasna human rights centre in Belarus in 1996. He was jailed for tax evasion from 2011 to 2014, arrested again on 14 July 2021 when Viasna's offices were raided, awarded the 2022 Nobel Peace Prize while in pre-trial detention, and sentenced on 3 March 2023 to ten years for smuggling and financing protests [[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ales_Bialiatski)]. He was released on 13 December 2025 with 122 other prisoners after the United States agreed to ease sanctions on Belarusian potash, and was put across the border into Lithuania [[Amnesty](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2025/12/belarus-long-overdue-release-of-maryia-kalesnikava-ales-bialiatski-and-others-must-not-mask-the-scale-of-repression/)]. Two other people released in the same deal are on this list.

## Andrzej Poczobut

![Andrzej Poczobut](/misc/media/writers-in-prison/poczobut.jpg)

**Country**: Belarus

**Known for**: Journalist, _Gazeta Wyborcza_ correspondent, and Polish-minority advocate

**Years in prison**: Mar 2021-Apr 2026

**Current status**: Released in a prisoner exchange in Apr 2026

**Imprisoned for**: Reporting on the 2020 protests and defending the Polish minority

Andrzej Poczobut is a Belarusian journalist of Polish descent, the Belarus correspondent of _Gazeta Wyborcza_ and deputy chairman of the Union of Poles in Belarus. He was given a suspended sentence for libelling Lukashenko in 2011, detained in the crackdown on the Union of Poles on 25 March 2021, and sentenced in February 2023 to eight years in a labour camp for "inciting hatred" and "calling for sanctions", meaning his reporting on the 2020 protests and his defence of the Polish minority [[CPJ](https://cpj.org/2026/04/belarus-frees-journalist-andrzej-poczobut/)]. He was denied medication in prison and had lost nineteen kilograms when he was handed over at the Polish border on 28 April 2026 in a ten-person exchange brokered by the United States. Poland gave him the Order of the White Eagle five days later. He shared the 2025 Sakharov Prize with the Georgian journalist Mzia Amaglobeli, who is still in prison.

## Arnon Nampa

![Arnon Nampa](/misc/media/writers-in-prison/nampa.jpg)

**Country**: Thailand

**Known for**: Human-rights lawyer and poet

**Years in prison**: In prison since Sep 2023

**Current status**: Imprisoned; appeals pending as of the source date

**Imprisoned for**: Lese-majeste convictions for speeches and social-media posts

Arnon Nampa is a Thai human rights lawyer and poet. On 3 August 2020, at a Harry Potter-themed rally, he became the first person in a generation to call for reform of the Thai monarchy from a public stage, and he said explicitly that he wanted it reformed, not abolished. Thailand has since convicted him under Section 112, the lèse-majesté law, in eleven separate cases for speeches and social media posts from 2020 and 2021, most recently on 20 February 2026, and the sentences add up to more than thirty years [[Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/world/2026/02/20/thai-court-adds-nearly-three-years-to-activist-lawyers-jail-term-for-monarchy-criticism/209757)]. He has been in prison since September 2023, the Supreme Court refused him bail on 25 December 2025 while his appeals are pending, and the Bangkok Criminal Court has pursued his disbarment on the ground that representing clients in a prison uniform breaches its dress code [PEN case list 2026]. He was shackled in court for his latest sentencing [[Front Line Defenders](https://www.frontlinedefenders.org/en/case/human-rights-lawyer-arnon-nampa-shackled-court-during-latest-sentencing)].

## Ashraf Omar

**Country**: Egypt

**Known for**: Editorial cartoonist and translator for Al-Manassa

**Years in prison**: In pre-trial detention since Jul 2024

**Current status**: Detained; trial listed for Oct 2026

**Imprisoned for**: Accused of financing terrorism and participating in a terrorist group

Ashraf Omar is an Egyptian editorial cartoonist and translator who drew for Al-Manassa about the sale of state assets and the electricity cuts. Security forces took him from his home on 22 July 2024, and the State Security Prosecution has renewed his pre-trial detention ever since [[CPJ](https://cpj.org/2026/07/cpj-partners-demand-release-of-egyptian-cartoonist-ashraf-omar-on-arrest-anniversary/)]. In November 2025 he and eleven others were referred to the Cairo Criminal Court for "financing terrorism" and "participation in a terrorist group". The trial was set for May 2026, then July, and is now set for 12 October 2026. His wife, Nada Mougheeth, was arrested in January 2025 and accused of membership in a terrorist group and spreading false news. No photograph of him under a licence that permits reuse exists.

## Boualem Sansal

![Boualem Sansal](/misc/media/writers-in-prison/sansal.jpg)

**Country**: Algeria

**Known for**: Franco-Algerian novelist; author of _2084_ and _The German Mujahid_

**Years in prison**: 361 days, Nov 2024-Nov 2025

**Current status**: Pardoned and transferred to Germany in Nov 2025

**Imprisoned for**: Undermining national unity over comments in an interview

Boualem Sansal is a Franco-Algerian novelist, the author of _2084_ and _The German Mujahid_, and a winner of the Peace Prize of the German Book Trade and the Académie française's Grand Prix du roman. Algeria detained him at Algiers airport on 16 November 2024, at the age of 75 and with prostate cancer, over an interview in which he said that France had given Morocco's territory to Algeria when it drew the colonial border. He was charged with undermining national unity, sentenced on 27 March 2025 to five years, and lost his appeal on 1 July 2025 [[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boualem_Sansal)]. Algeria pardoned him in November 2025 after Germany's president asked, 361 days after his arrest, and flew him to a hospital in Germany. The Académie française elected him on 29 January 2026 with 25 votes out of 26 [[franceinfo](https://www.franceinfo.fr/culture/livres/boualem-sansal/boualem-sansal-elu-a-l-academie-francaise-devenir-academicien-c-est-entrer-dans-l-histoire-de-france-confie-l-ecrivain_7772798.html)].

## Chow Hang-tung

![Chow Hang-tung](/misc/media/writers-in-prison/chow.jpg)

**Country**: Hong Kong

**Known for**: Barrister and former vice-chair of the Hong Kong Alliance

**Years in prison**: In custody since Sep 2021

**Current status**: Convicted on national-security charges; sentencing pending in the source

**Imprisoned for**: Tiananmen vigil activity and alleged incitement to subversion

Chow Hang-tung is a Hong Kong barrister and was vice-chair of the Hong Kong Alliance, which held the Victoria Park vigil for the Tiananmen dead every 4 June for thirty years. She was convicted for the 2020 and 2021 vigils, which were banned, and served those sentences. She has been in custody since September 2021, refused bail on the national security charge of inciting subversion, and she represented herself. On 21 August 2026 the High Court convicted her, Lee Cheuk-yan and the Alliance itself; the judgment treated the Alliance's slogan "end one-party dictatorship" as an attempt to undermine the Communist Party [[HKFP](https://hongkongfp.com/2026/08/21/breaking-hong-kong-tiananmen-vigil-activists-convicted-under-nat-security-law-face-up-to-10-years-prison/)]. The judge interrupted her mitigation on 28 August and she published the full text online [[HKFP](https://hongkongfp.com/2026/08/28/in-full-tiananmen-vigil-activist-chow-hang-tungs-mitigation-after-guilty-verdict-in-national-security-trial/)]. Sentencing is pending and the maximum is ten years. She was separately arrested in May 2024 for social media posts under the Safeguarding National Security Ordinance.

## Dawit Isaak

![Placards at a demonstration for Dawit Isaak in Stockholm, 2015](/misc/media/writers-in-prison/isaak.jpg)

**Country**: Eritrea

**Known for**: Playwright and co-owner of the independent newspaper _Setit_

**Years in prison**: Since Sep 2001; nearly 25 years by Sep 2026

**Current status**: Detained without charge or trial; no family contact for years

**Imprisoned for**: Arrested in the crackdown following an open letter calling for reform

Dawit Isaak is a playwright, a Swedish citizen since 1992, and was part-owner of _Setit_, Eritrea's first independent newspaper. He was arrested at his home in Asmara on 23 September 2001 in the crackdown that followed an open letter for reform signed by fifteen members of the government, and _Setit_ was closed the same day [[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawit_Isaak)]. He has never been charged or tried. He was let out for two days in November 2005 to see a doctor and rearrested on the way to the hospital. He has had no contact with his family in that time. May 2026 was his nine-thousandth day in detention and 23 September 2026 is the twenty-fifth anniversary [[Raoul Wallenberg Centre](https://www.raoulwallenbergcentre.org/en/news/2026-05-15)]. Sweden's foreign minister visited Eritrea in December 2025 and came back without proof that he is alive.

## Dong Yuyu

**Country**: China

**Known for**: Journalist, editor, Nieman fellow, and former _New York Times_ Chinese-edition columnist

**Years in prison**: Since Feb 2022; seven-year sentence imposed in Nov 2024

**Current status**: Imprisoned at Chaobai Prison in Tianjin

**Imprisoned for**: Espionage, after meeting a Japanese diplomat

Dong Yuyu was deputy head of the editorial department at _Guangming Daily_, a state newspaper, a Nieman fellow, and a columnist for the Chinese edition of the _New York Times_. He was detained on 21 February 2022 while having lunch with a Japanese diplomat at a Beijing hotel, held for six months under "residential surveillance at a designated location", charged with espionage in March 2023, and sentenced in November 2024 to seven years [[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_Yuyu)]. The Beijing High People's Court rejected his appeal on 13 November 2025 without giving reasons. Through his family he said the case was about warning others not to engage with the outside world. He was moved from Beijing No. 2 Prison to Chaobai Prison in Tianjin on 17 March 2026.

## Eskinder Nega

![Eskinder Nega](/misc/media/writers-in-prison/nega.jpg)

**Country**: Ethiopia

**Known for**: Journalist and newspaper founder

**Years in prison**: Seventeen months after 2005; about seven years from 2011-2018; detained again 2020-2022

**Current status**: Released; later became involved in the Fano insurgency

**Imprisoned for**: Election-related journalism and later anti-terrorism charges tied to a column about the Arab Spring

Eskinder Nega is an Ethiopian journalist who founded four newspapers. He was imprisoned for seventeen months after the 2005 election, sentenced in 2012 to eighteen years under the anti-terrorism law for a column about the Arab Spring, and released in 2018 after seven years, with the PEN/Barbara Goldsmith award and the Golden Pen of Freedom collected in his absence [[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eskinder_Nega)]. He was arrested again in June 2020 and released in January 2022. In 2023 he went to the Amhara region and became a commander in the Fano insurgency against the government that had jailed him, and in 2024 an attempt was made to put all of Fano under his leadership, which several factions rejected. In January 2026 he declined a politburo seat in the merged Amhara Fano National Movement and said he would serve as an ordinary member.

## Floriane Irangabiye

**Country**: Burundi

**Known for**: Journalist and co-founder of Radio Igicaniro

**Years in prison**: About two years, Aug 2022-Aug 2024

**Current status**: Pardoned and released in Aug 2024

**Imprisoned for**: Undermining territorial integrity after a program critical of the government

Floriane Irangabiye is a Burundian journalist who co-founded Radio Igicaniro, an online station run from Rwanda by exiles. She went home in August 2022 and was intercepted on the way to a parent's funeral, after a programme in which guests criticised the government. On 2 January 2023 she was convicted of undermining the integrity of the national territory and sentenced to ten years and a fine, and the appeal court upheld it in May [[CPJ](https://cpj.org/2024/08/burundian-journalist-floriane-irangabiye-released-after-two-years-in-prison/)]. President Ndayishimiye pardoned her on 14 August 2024 and she was released from Bubanza prison two days later.

## Galal El-Behairy

**Country**: Egypt

**Known for**: Poet and lyricist

**Years in prison**: Since Mar 2018; ninth birthday in prison by Jun 2026

**Current status**: Imprisoned / repeatedly held in renewed pre-trial detention

**Imprisoned for**: Poetry and lyrics deemed insulting to the army or spreading false news; later terrorism-related accusations

Galal El-Behairy is an Egyptian poet and lyricist. He wrote the words to Ramy Essam's song "Balaha", which mocked Sisi, and was arrested on 5 March 2018, a week after it came out. The civilian prosecution over the song was dropped; a military court then tried him over an unpublished poetry collection, _The Finest Women on Earth_, for insulting the army and spreading false news, and gave him three years [[PEN America](https://pen.org/writer-at-risk/galal-el-behairy/)]. When the sentence ended in July 2021 he was disappeared for three weeks and then put back into pre-trial detention on a new case, which by 2025 had run past every limit Egyptian law sets for pre-trial detention. He went on hunger strike in March 2023 and again in September 2023, and attempted suicide on 9 September 2023. In August 2025 the State Security Prosecution opened two more cases against him, one for communicating with terrorist groups, based on a 2023 National Security report [PEN case list 2026]. He received PEN America's Freedom to Write Award in May 2025. His thirty-sixth birthday, on 27 June 2026, was his ninth in prison.

## Gautam Navlakha

![Gautam Navlakha](/misc/media/writers-in-prison/navlakha.jpg)

**Country**: India

**Known for**: Journalist and civil-rights activist

**Years in prison**: Apr 2020-Nov 2022 in jail, then house arrest until bail in May 2024

**Current status**: On bail; trial had not started in the source

**Imprisoned for**: Bhima Koregaon UAPA case alleging involvement in a Maoist plot

Gautam Navlakha is an Indian journalist, an editorial consultant at the _Economic and Political Weekly_, and a former secretary of the People's Union for Democratic Rights. He is one of sixteen people arrested under the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act over a rally at Bhima Koregaon in January 2018, on the theory that they were part of a Maoist plot against the prime minister [[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Bhima_Koregaon_violence)]. Arsenal Consulting, an American forensics firm, found that the letters the plot rests on were planted on two co-accused's computers by an attacker, and SentinelOne traced the attacker to Pune police. Navlakha surrendered to the National Investigation Agency in April 2020, was moved to house arrest in November 2022, and was bailed by the Supreme Court on 14 May 2024, on condition that he pay two million rupees for the cost of the guards who had held him. The trial has not started. Another of the sixteen, Stan Swamy, an 84-year-old priest with Parkinson's, died in custody in 2021.

## Gui Minhai

**Country**: China

**Known for**: Poet, publisher, and co-owner of Causeway Bay Books / Mighty Current

**Years in prison**: Detained 2015-2017, then again from Jan 2018; ten-year sentence from 2020

**Current status**: Imprisoned; Sweden denied consular access

**Imprisoned for**: Illegally providing intelligence abroad; earlier detention followed politically sensitive publishing

Gui Minhai is a Swedish citizen, poet and publisher, co-owner of Causeway Bay Books in Hong Kong and of Mighty Current, which published gossip about China's leaders. He was taken from his holiday flat in Pattaya, Thailand, on 17 October 2015, and appeared on Chinese state television three months later confessing to a 2003 traffic accident [[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gui_Minhai)]. He was released under surveillance in October 2017, and on 20 January 2018 plainclothes police pulled him off a train to Beijing on which he was travelling with two Swedish diplomats to a medical appointment for a neurodegenerative disease. In February 2020 he was sentenced to ten years for illegally providing intelligence abroad, and China announced that he had resumed Chinese citizenship, which is used to deny Sweden consular access. In August 2025 the UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention called for his release [PEN case list 2026].

## Ilham Tohti

![Ilham Tohti](/misc/media/writers-in-prison/tohti.jpg)

**Country**: China

**Known for**: Uyghur economist and founder of Uyghur Online

**Years in prison**: Since Jan 2014; life sentence

**Current status**: Imprisoned, believed to be in Urumqi No. 1 Prison

**Imprisoned for**: Separatism, despite advocating dialogue and opposing separatism

Ilham Tohti is a Uyghur economist who taught at Minzu University in Beijing and ran Uyghur Online, a site for discussion between Uyghurs and Han in both languages. He argued for dialogue and against separatism, in writing, for years. He was detained on 15 January 2014, tried for two days in September, convicted of separatism and sentenced to life [[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilham_Tohti)]. Seven of his students were sentenced to between three and a half and eight years for helping with the website. He received the Martin Ennals Award in 2016, and the Václav Havel Prize and the Sakharov Prize in 2019, accepted by his daughter, who said she did not know where her father was. Nobody in his family has seen him since 2017, when the quarterly prison visits stopped, and when they call the prison nobody answers [[ISHR](https://ishr.org/ilham-tohti/)]. He is believed to be in Urumqi No. 1 Prison.

## Innocent Bahati

**Country**: Rwanda

**Known for**: Poet and technology teacher

**Years in prison**: Disappeared since Feb 2021; prior three-month detention in 2017

**Current status**: Whereabouts unknown; government denies holding him

**Imprisoned for**: No formal charge disclosed; disappearance followed critical poetry and a prior Facebook comment

Innocent Bahati is a Rwandan poet who performed on YouTube about poverty, refugees and the church, and taught technology at a school in Kigali. On 7 February 2021 he went to dinner with an unidentified person at a hotel in Nyanza district and did not come back; his phone was off that evening [[PEN](https://www.pen-international.org/news/rwanda-investigate-and-publicly-account-for-the-whereabouts-of-poet-innocent-bahati)]. The Rwanda Investigation Bureau said it was not holding him and that an investigation was ongoing. Since then the government's answers have included a promise of a full report, and a claim that he left to join an armed group in Uganda. He had disappeared once before, in 2017, after a Facebook comment, and reappeared in police custody, where he was held for three months without charge. More than a hundred writers, including Atwood, Coetzee and Okri, signed a letter to Kagame about him. As of September 2026, he had been missing for five and a half years.

## Jimmy Lai

![Jimmy Lai](/misc/media/writers-in-prison/lai.jpg)

**Country**: Hong Kong

**Known for**: Founder of Next Digital and _Apple Daily_

**Years in prison**: Held since Dec 2020; twenty-year sentence imposed Feb 2026

**Current status**: Imprisoned

**Imprisoned for**: Collusion with foreign forces and conspiracy to publish seditious material

Jimmy Lai founded the clothing chain Giordano, then Next Digital, then _Apple Daily_, the largest pro-democracy newspaper in Hong Kong. He is a British citizen. He was arrested under the national security law on 10 August 2020, _Apple Daily_ was raided and its accounts frozen until it closed in June 2021, and he has been held since December 2020, in solitary confinement, which the government says is at his own request [[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Lai)]. On 15 December 2025 he was convicted of two counts of conspiracy to collude with foreign forces and one of conspiracy to publish seditious material, and on 9 February 2026 he was sentenced to twenty years, twice the previous record under the law [[CPJ](https://cpj.org/2026/02/jimmy-lai-sentenced-to-20-years-in-prison-in-hong-kongs-biggest-media-trial/)]. He is 78 and diabetic. On 26 February 2026 the Court of Appeal quashed his earlier fraud conviction, which was over the terms of an office lease. His lawyers say he will not appeal the security conviction.

## Katsiaryna Andreyeva

![Katsiaryna Andreyeva](/misc/media/writers-in-prison/andreyeva.jpg)

**Country**: Belarus

**Known for**: Belsat journalist

**Years in prison**: Nov 2020-Mar 2026

**Current status**: Pardoned, released, and sent to Lithuania in Mar 2026

**Imprisoned for**: Livestreaming a police crackdown; later convicted of treason

Katsiaryna Andreyeva is a reporter for Belsat, the Polish-based Belarusian channel. On 15 November 2020 she and her colleague Darya Chultsova were arrested while livestreaming from a Minsk flat as police broke up a memorial for Raman Bandarenka, who had been beaten to death days earlier. They were sentenced in February 2021 to two years for organising actions that violate public order, the actions being the livestream [[CPJ](https://cpj.org/2026/03/belarusian-journalist-katsiaryna-andreyeva-released-after-serving-over-5-years-in-prison/)]. In July 2022 she was tried again, behind closed doors, for treason, and given eight more years. She was pardoned in March 2026 with 249 other prisoners after the United States lifted sanctions on Belarusian potash and banks, and was among fifteen put over the border into Lithuania without a passport. She said that if she had been given a choice she would have stayed in Belarus.

## Khadija Ismayilova

![Khadija Ismayilova](/misc/media/writers-in-prison/ismayilova.jpg)

**Country**: Azerbaijan

**Known for**: Investigative journalist known for reporting on the Aliyev family

**Years in prison**: Dec 2014-May 2016

**Current status**: Released on probation; remained under a travel ban in the source

**Imprisoned for**: Tax evasion and abuse of power; ECHR later found retaliation for journalism

Khadija Ismayilova is an Azerbaijani investigative journalist, formerly of Radio Free Europe, who documented the Aliyev family's offshore companies, mines and hotels. In 2012 she was sent stills from a camera hidden in her bedroom with a demand that she stop, and she published the demand instead [[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khadija_Ismayilova)]. She was arrested in December 2014 for inciting a colleague's suicide, acquitted of that, and convicted instead of tax evasion and abuse of power, for seven and a half years. She was released on probation in May 2016 and kept under a travel ban, which is why the UNESCO press freedom prize and the Right Livelihood Award were collected without her. In January 2026 the European Court of Human Rights found that the prosecution was retaliation for her journalism and violated four articles of the Convention [[CPJ](https://cpj.org/2026/01/european-court-rules-azerbaijan-prosecuted-khadija-ismayilova-in-retaliation-for-journalism/)]. She is editor-in-chief of Toplum TV, whose office was raided and six staff detained in March 2024. She still lives in Baku.

## Mahvash Sabet

**Country**: Iran

**Known for**: Teacher, poet, and former administrator of the Baha'i Institute for Higher Education

**Years in prison**: Ten years served after the 2008 arrest; Jul 2022-Dec 2024 in custody before medical leave

**Current status**: At home on medical grounds, under a travel ban and liable to return to prison

**Imprisoned for**: Espionage and propaganda charges tied to Baha'i activity

Mahvash Sabet is an Iranian teacher and poet, a Baha'i, who ran the Baha'i Institute for Higher Education for fifteen years because Baha'is are barred from Iranian universities. She was the first of the seven Baha'i leaders arrested in 2008, was sentenced to twenty years for espionage and propaganda, reduced to ten, and served all of it [[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahvash_Sabet)]. She began writing poetry in prison; _Prison Poems_ was published in English in 2013 and she was PEN's International Writer of Courage in 2017. She was rearrested on 31 July 2022, beaten during interrogation, held in solitary for 42 days, and given a second ten years in November 2023 after a one-hour trial. She has pulmonary fibrosis and heart disease, had open-heart surgery in December 2024, and has been at home on medical grounds since, under a travel ban and liable to be returned to prison [PEN case list 2026]. Her memoir _Open Wide the Doors_ came out in July 2026. She is 73.

## Maksim Znak

![Maksim Znak](/misc/media/writers-in-prison/znak.jpg)

**Country**: Belarus

**Known for**: Lawyer for opposition presidential campaigns and Coordination Council member

**Years in prison**: Sep 2020-Dec 2025

**Current status**: Released in Dec 2025 and sent abroad

**Imprisoned for**: Conspiracy to seize power and leading an extremist organisation

Maksim Znak is a Belarusian lawyer who represented the presidential candidate Viktar Babaryka and then Sviatlana Tsikhanouskaya, and sat on the Coordination Council formed after the 2020 election. On 9 September 2020 masked men in plain clothes took him from his office; he managed to text one word, "masks", to colleagues. He was tried behind closed doors with Maryia Kalesnikava and sentenced on 6 September 2021 to ten years for conspiracy to seize power and leading an extremist organisation [[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxim_Znak)]. His lawyer last saw him in February 2023. In prison he wrote _Zekameron_, a hundred stories from the cells. He was released on 13 December 2025 in the same deal as Bialiatski and Kalesnikava, sent first to Ukraine and then to Poland.

## Maryia Kalesnikava

![Maryia Kalesnikava](/misc/media/writers-in-prison/kalesnikava.png)

**Country**: Belarus

**Known for**: Musician, campaign manager, and Coordination Council member

**Years in prison**: Sep 2020-Dec 2025

**Current status**: Released in Dec 2025 and taken to Germany

**Imprisoned for**: Conspiracy to seize power

Maryia Kalesnikava is a flautist and conductor who ran Viktar Babaryka's presidential campaign in Belarus in 2020 and then joined the Coordination Council. On 7 September 2020 she was abducted in Minsk and driven to the Ukrainian border to be deported; in the neutral zone she climbed out of the car's rear window, tore up her passport, and walked back into Belarus, where she was arrested [[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Kalesnikava)]. On 6 September 2021 she was sentenced to eleven years for conspiracy to seize power. In November 2022, after being put in a punishment cell, she was operated on for a perforated ulcer. Nobody heard from her between February 2023 and November 2024, when her father was allowed a visit. She was released on 13 December 2025 with 122 others and taken to Germany, where she performed again in April 2026 [[Amnesty](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2025/12/belarus-long-overdue-release-of-maryia-kalesnikava-ales-bialiatski-and-others-must-not-mask-the-scale-of-repression/)].

## Maykel Osorbo

![Maykel Osorbo](/misc/media/writers-in-prison/osorbo.jpg)

**Country**: Cuba

**Known for**: Rapper and performer on "Patria y Vida"

**Years in prison**: Since May 2021; nine-year sentence

**Current status**: Imprisoned at Guanajay maximum-security prison

**Imprisoned for**: Contempt, assault, public disorder, and defamation of institutions

Maykel Castillo Pérez, who records as Maykel Osorbo, is a Cuban rapper and one of the voices on "Patria y Vida", the song that turned the regime's slogan around and won two Latin Grammys in 2021. He has been held since 18 May 2021, and on 24 June 2022 was sentenced to nine years for contempt, assault, public disorder and defamation of institutions [[Amnesty](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2026/06/cuba-a-cuatro-anos-de-una-condena-injusta-artistas-afrocubanos-siguen-encarcelados-por-expresars/)]. In 2025 he was put in a punishment cell in April, July, August, September and October, and went on hunger strike in June; his family are not allowed his medical records [PEN case list 2026]. In May 2026 State Security offered him exile or prison until 2030, then withdrew the offer. The United States has granted him humanitarian parole. He was moved to the maximum-security prison at Guanajay on 10 July 2026 and turned 43 there in August. His co-defendant Luis Manuel Otero Alcántara was released into exile in Miami.

## Yuri Dmitriev

![Yuri Dmitriev](/misc/media/writers-in-prison/dmitriev.jpg)

**Country**: Russia

**Known for**: Historian who documented Stalin-era mass graves, including Sandarmokh

**Years in prison**: Since Dec 2016 apart from the period around his 2018 acquittal; fifteen-year sentence

**Current status**: Imprisoned in a maximum-security colony in Mordovia

**Imprisoned for**: Sexual-abuse-related charges arising from medical photographs; acquittal overturned and sentence repeatedly increased

Yuri Dmitriev is a historian who ran the Karelian branch of Memorial and spent thirty years finding the graves of people shot under Stalin, including the mass grave at Sandarmokh, where more than six thousand were killed in 1937 and 1938. He was arrested on 13 December 2016 and charged with making pornographic images of his adopted daughter, then eleven; the images were the medical photographs he had been told to keep by social services to document her health. He was acquitted in April 2018. The acquittal was overturned, he was rearrested and given a new charge, sentenced to three and a half years in July 2020, then thirteen years on a closed appeal without him or his lawyer present, then fifteen [[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yury_Dmitriev)]. He turned 70 on 28 January 2026 in a maximum-security colony in Mordovia, with suspected cancer for which he has been refused tests for three years, and he is put in solitary for sitting on his bed during the day [[Moscow Times](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/01/28/freeing-the-historian-yury-dmitriyev-is-a-matter-of-life-and-death-a91785)].

Zviad Ratiani is a Georgian poet and the translator of Rilke, Eliot, Pound and Celan into Georgian.
